Why $\omega_1 T_2 > 1$ matters

The transition from CW to pulsed magnetic resonance — and equivalently from CW DNP to pulsed DNP — hinges on whether the spin can complete a coherent rotation before transverse relaxation damps it out. Quantitatively, this is the inequality $$ \omega_1 T_2 > 1, $$ where $\omega_1$ is the RF/microwave Rabi frequency and $T_2$ is the transverse relaxation time.

  • CW regime ($\omega_1 T_2 \ll 1$): $M_x, M_y$ stay small (of order $\omega_1 T_2$). $M_z$ decays monotonically toward a saturated steady state. No oscillations are visible — the dynamics are dominated by relaxation.
  • Pulsed regime ($\omega_1 T_2 \gg 1$): the magnetization undergoes many Rabi cycles before relaxation acts, so coherences $M_{x,y}$ are large and oscillatory. This is the regime where pulse sequences (NOVEL, ISE, etc.) can transfer polarization efficiently before $T_2$ decay.

For pulsed DNP to operate as something genuinely distinct from CW DNP — analogous to the Ernst–Anderson transition from CW NMR to FT NMR — the microwave hardware must satisfy $\omega_{1e} T_{2e} > 1$ on the electron spin.
